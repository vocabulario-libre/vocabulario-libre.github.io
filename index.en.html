Lecturas de los libertadores

Fifteen texts by three liberators: Bolívar, San Martín, O’Higgins. About forty thousand words from each, 1812–1843: manifestos, speeches, proclamations, dispatches and official letters, public and private letters, maxims. Chronological order. The text is public domain; everything else — editions, apparatus, glosses, programs — is CC0: copy it, host it, adapt it, no condition.

Status, from the governing document (Spanish): Corpus cerrado y publicado (2 de noviembre de 2026).

Study

Léxico, the vocabulary trainer.

  • Every word form the authors wrote, close to thirty thousand, each with the sentence it appears in and an English equivalent; over a thousand Spanish definitions for the hard words. The equivalent follows the word’s sense in that text, not the dictionary’s. Forms, not lemmas: the form is what the reader meets on the page.
  • One text at a time, or all fifteen. The author’s text and the edition’s apparatus, kept apart.
  • Decks: the whole lexicon; the edition’s glossary; by frequency; by paragraph range.
  • Modes: recognise, gap, produce, index. The interface is in Spanish.
  • Spaced repetition. Progress is saved in the browser and exported to a file.
  • One file. No network, no install.

The texts

The standalone pieces were chosen on two criteria of the compiler’s: historical weight and prose. The correspondences and the printed papers, by unit — the whole series to one correspondent, the whole register of one witness, a date window fixed before the pages were read; nothing is omitted inside a unit. Each author has public prose from the campaign years and private letters from his last decades; the word count is reported, not engineered. Only what has a public-domain printed witness, read on the page image, enters. What was considered and not adopted is in the manifest, with its reason. Two of O’Higgins’s texts are not his hand — the 1818 manifesto, drafted by another under his name; the 1823 speech, transmitted by later account; recorded, not corrected. The editions record: they declare their witnesses, note every intervention, pass no judgement.

Each edition: the text, read on the page image against a dated printed witness; the note on the text; the apparatus — restitutions, corrections of reading, divergences, normalisation — with every intervention listed one by one; notes on the sources; a reference key; a glossary; a revision history; numbered paragraphs. “Source” is that text in Markdown, from which the PDF and EPUB are generated. “Register” is the list of checks the edition passed — declaratory, methodological, textual, epistemic, reproducibility — with the proof of each. The editions, the trainer’s interface and the governing document are in Spanish; the glosses, the policy and the README are in English. These are reading editions for private study, not critical editions, and should not be cited as established text.

Mirror

This folder is mirrored by copying it whole. To verify it: sha256sum -c MANIFEST.sha256 inside lecturas/. To rebuild it: the programs included in the folder, on Python’s standard library and reportlab, as the README says: every PDF and every EPUB rebuilds identical, byte for byte, from the source text. The page carries no script and no tracking; the trainer runs only inside its own file; there are no accounts. The site is a folder of files.
